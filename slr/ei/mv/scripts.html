Slide 3: Introduction: Structural Context & Core Paradox

Time Target: 3.0 min Word Count: 215

Slide 3 outlines the structural context and our core economic paradox.

On the theoretical side, we map out the foundational tension between Kirznerian Discovery Theory and Schumpeterian Creation Theory. Kirznerian discovery relies on individual alertness to exploit existing market inefficiencies through imitation. However, an over-reliance on imitation risks rapid market saturation. To catalyze deep industrial transformation, we need radical Schumpeterian creative destruction—shifting focus toward innovation-oriented venture creation.

As a government software programmer, my positionality is grounded in data analytics. I am a scholar-practitioner looking at why massive, macro-level technical infrastructure investments face critical bottlenecks when scaling down to micro-level human execution.

This brings us to the Hong Kong–GBA Institutional Matrix. In 2026, Hong Kong's macro-ecosystem shows powerful structural growth, with over 4,600 active startups. Yet, here lies our core institutional paradox: this macro-capitalization fails to activate organic student venture creation.

Under Institutional Theory, Hong Kong's lucrative corporate career pathways artificially inflate a university graduate's reservation wage. When weighed against exorbitant local living costs and risk-averse cultural frictions, our premium youth talent faces immense opportunity costs. They rationally choose secure corporate trajectories over high-uncertainty startups. Therefore, this dissertation introduces an affective engine to bridge this gap.

Slide 4: Introduction: Problem Statement & Research Design

Time Target: 2.5 min Word Count: 190

Building on this paradox, Slide 4 introduces our Problem Statement and Mixed-Methods Research Design.

Existing regional literature relies on a "cold cognitive hyper-focus." By repeatedly applying the traditional Theory of Planned Behaviour, current studies verify that students have the intent to create, but fail to explain why those intentions stall. Moving from an idea to actual market entry requires an Affective Engine. Entrepreneurial Passion—specifically for inventing and founding—is the psychological fuel required to override high corporate reservation wages. Furthermore, macro-frameworks like Knowledge Spillover Theory have near-zero empirical representation in local student data.

To resolve this, our dual-phase design explicitly deconstructs the mechanisms that translate passive student intentions into active market entries.

  • Phase 1 (Quantitative Core): We deploy PLS-SEM via SmartPLS 4, targeting at least 135 respondents. This addresses Research Questions 1, 2, and 3, statistically mapping how individual traits trigger passion, and how Perceived Ecosystem Support moderates the path to action.
  • Phase 2 (Qualitative Nuance): Through inductive thematic analysis of 10 to 20 semi-structured interviews with nascent innovators, we address Research Question 4, uncovering specific cultural risk thresholds and face-saving barriers.

Slide 5: Chapter 2 Overview & SLR Methodology

Time Target: 2.0 min Word Count: 145

Moving into Chapter 2, Slide 5 establishes the architecture of our Systematic Literature Review (SLR).

Following Denyer and Tranfield’s CIMO methodology, we searched EBSCO, Scopus, and Web of Science. We used a two-tiered filtering strategy: first, a quality-first extraction restricted to elite AJG 4-star journals to capture dominant paradigms; second, a targeted grey-literature sweep to eliminate Western publication bias. This yielded a final sample of n = 285 papers refined via the PRISMA protocol.

Our bibliometric analytics reveal a profound empirical vacuum in the Hong Kong corpus. While global studies extensively utilize advanced longitudinal datasets, local studies remain trapped in cross-sectional surveys. More critically, foundational paradigms like Human Capital Theory, Knowledge Spillover Theory, and the Schumpeterian versus Kirznerian debate possess exactly zero empirical representation within the Hong Kong student corpus.

Slide 6: Theoretical Evolution — Cognitive Intent to Affective Drive

Time Target: 2.0 min Word Count: 145

Slide 6 charts the epistemological evolution from cold cognitive intent models to dynamic, affective behavioral drivers.

Evaluating student career selection traditionally relied on purely cognitive frameworks like Ajzen’s Theory of Planned Behaviour. However, regional scholarship demonstrates that these psychometric predictors undergo severe distortions in collectivist environments like Hong Kong, where individual confidence is heavily constrained by relational, face-saving societal expectations.

To resolve this, we infuse behavioral economics to model the venture choice as a psychological conflict against job selection boundaries. In a stable financial hub like Hong Kong, secure corporate pathways inflate a graduate’s reservation wage. To execute real market entry, a nascent student requires specific cognitive distortions to override this gravity. Following Nobre's taxonomy, these include optimism, overconfidence, and loss aversion. Crucially, these distortions are fueled by the Affective Engine of Entrepreneurial Passion.

Slide 7: Contextual Paradox — HK Ecosystem vs. Stanford Benchmark

Time Target: 2.0 min Word Count: 140

Slide 7 benchmarks Hong Kong's ecosystem against the high-density Western archetype of the Stanford University incubation system.

Stanford graduates also face exceptionally high corporate reservation wages in Silicon Valley. However, the Stanford ecosystem successfully neutralizes this pull through its structural configuration: an unmatched concentration of venture capital, mature Technology Transfer Offices (TTOs), dense academic-corporate knowledge spillovers, and zero social stigma regarding failure.

In sharp contrast, Hong Kong’s commercial legacy has historically favored a conservative, Kirznerian approach focused on short-term financial flexibility, property speculation, and imitative arbitrage. Radical Schumpeterian technological creation is heavily constrained by immense real estate friction, intense family face-saving pressures, and deficits in specialized, long-term seed resources. Student accelerators face severe structural trade-offs, proving that passion cannot act in a vacuum; it requires targeted structural interventions.

Slide 8: Institutional Catalysts & Multi-Stakeholder Alignment

Time Target: 2.0 min Word Count: 145

To break this regional inertia, Slide 8 introduces macro-environmental catalysts and micro-foundational alignments.

At the macro level, Hong Kong's integration into the Greater Bay Area (GBA) innovation corridor fundamentally alters knowledge transfer. It bypasses localized land and supply-chain constraints by combining Hong Kong's elite academic research capacity with the rapid manufacturing ecosystem of mainland China.

Moving to the micro-foundations of stakeholder support, large-scale dataset analyses confirm that instrumental support from immediate networks facilitates the transition from student to active founder. Our model explicitly evaluates Perceived Ecosystem Support across five distinct archetypes—ranging from university science parks and accelerators to campus TTOs. We analyze how balancing institutional integration with the founder’s operational autonomy, combined with Work-Integrated Learning and self-determination, can close the intent–action gap.

Slide 9: Methodology — The Mixed-Methods Architecture

Time Target: 2.0 min Word Count: 125

Chapter 3 details our explanatory sequential mixed-methods architecture, designed to decode the statistical boundaries of our quantitative model through qualitative insight.

In Phase 1 (The Quantitative Core), we deploy a deductive approach to validate the structural paths of our Passion Ecosystem Model. Using PLS-SEM within SmartPLS 4, we compute the relationships between individual traits, affective passion, and environmental moderators among a target sample of n ≥ 135.

In Phase 2 (The Nuance Engine), we shift to an inductive approach. While quantitative data shows what is significant, it cannot capture the underlying anxieties of a student facing a high reservation wage. Using thematic analysis via NVivo, we explore the lived experiences of 10 to 20 nascent innovators, using purposive sampling brokered via the HKMU alumni network.
